# How to label sentiment

For each sentence, decide: is the writer saying something **good**, something **bad**, or **nothing either way**?

Ask yourself: *if a teammate posted this in Slack, would I read it as good news, bad news, or just plain information?*

## Positive

The sentence is about something that went well, or the writer sounds happy, relieved, or proud.

- "The deployment to production went smoothly, no issues." — went well
- "The code review was fast and the comments were really helpful." — praise
- "We finally got automatic deployment working." — relief

## Negative

The sentence is about something that went badly, or the writer sounds annoyed, frustrated, or unhappy.

- "The deployment crashed in the middle of the night." — went badly
- "They invited me to another meeting that could've been an email." — complaint
- "We missed every goal this quarter and management isn't happy." — bad news

## Neutral

The sentence has **no good or bad feeling at all** — it's just a fact, a schedule, or a description.

- "The next deployment is planned for Tuesday morning." — just a schedule
- "We have a status meeting every Monday." — just a fact
- "Let's do a quick sync before the meeting." — just logistics

## The most common mistake

Don't pick "neutral" just because a sentence *sounds* calm or factual. Look at what actually happened in the sentence.

Example: "The build failed because of a dependency that changed without us knowing." This sounds calm, but something broke — that's **negative**, not neutral.

Rule of thumb: if the sentence describes something that happened, ask "was that a good thing or a bad thing?" Only mark it neutral if there's truly no good or bad outcome to judge.

## A few more tips

- **It doesn't matter if the sentence is in Hebrew, English, or mixed.** Judge the meaning, not the language.
- **You'll see similar sentences more than once.** They're the same idea written a few different ways. Label each one on its own — don't try to remember what you picked last time.
- **Go with your first instinct.** If you're stuck for more than a few seconds, just pick your gut answer and move on.
- **Take breaks.** Labeling 420 sentences in one sitting will make you tired and less careful. You can stop and come back anytime — your progress is saved automatically.
